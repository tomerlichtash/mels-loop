Lewis W. Imm was born in Milford, Nebraska, in 1905, and spent his early years on his father's cattle ranch in South Dakota. He attended the University of Minnesota for two years, majoring in mechanical engineering, and completed his studies at the University of Nebraska, graduating in 1927 with a degree in aeronautical engineering.

His first engineering position was with a Nebraska aeronautical firm, followed by employment with several small aircraft companies — highlighted by a typical Imm display of ingenuity: he was one of the first to convert an automobile engine into an aircraft engine. In 1935 he accepted a post at the Bureau of Air Commerce in Washington, D.C., and soon transferred to California. In 1937 came the invention the company grew from: a "garage-built" balance computer — the first "Librascope" — built to determine the center of balance for airplanes.

Throughout his career at Librascope, Imm put his personnel philosophy into action. That philosophy — which places the greatest importance on the employee as an individual — became a hallmark in the industry. His drive and devotion to the company showed in his participation in practically every phase of its operation: until the administrative and policy-making demands of the presidency occupied more and more of his time, he took an active part in the complete spectrum of company operations, from the design and delivery of a new product to employment interviewing and the development of new production equipment.

His interest in civic and youth activities never flagged. Membership in the Glendale Chamber of Commerce and the Future Engineers of America, and five consecutive terms as Los Angeles secretary of the Junior Achievement organization, are typical of these interests. In recognition of his personal contributions in the fields of fire control techniques and naval ordnance engineering, Imm received in 1957 the highest Navy civilian citation — the Navy Distinguished Public Service Award.

The father of two sons, Imm resided in Encino with his wife, Helen. He died at his home in Cottage Grove, Oregon, on January 22, 1992, at the age of 86.

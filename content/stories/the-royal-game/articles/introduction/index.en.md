Stefan Zweig's _The Royal Game_ (Schachnovelle) is widely considered one of the finest novellas of the twentieth century. Written in Brazilian exile in 1941, it was the last work Zweig completed before his death.

The story interweaves two obsessions with chess: that of the reigning world champion Mirko Czentovic, a man of limited intellect but extraordinary board intuition, and Dr. B., a cultured Viennese lawyer whose mind was both saved and nearly destroyed by the game during months of solitary confinement by the Gestapo.

## Themes

The novella explores the tension between mechanical calculation and creative imagination, between brute-force mastery and elegant understanding. These themes resonate deeply with the history of computing and artificial intelligence.

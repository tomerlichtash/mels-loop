Isaac Asimov wrote _The Last Question_ in 1956, and it remained his personal favorite among all his works. When asked which of his stories he liked best, he would invariably name this one.

The story spans the entire history of the universe, from the near future to its ultimate end, asking the same question at each stage: "Can entropy be reversed?" Each time, the ever-more-powerful computer answers: "INSUFFICIENT DATA FOR MEANINGFUL ANSWER."

## Why It Matters

_The Last Question_ is remarkable for its scope. In fewer than 5,000 words, Asimov traverses trillions of years, the heat death of the universe, and the nature of existence itself. It is a meditation on humanity's relationship with its tools, the limits of knowledge, and the possibility of transcendence through computation.

## Connection to Computing History

The story's central computer, Multivac, was inspired by the real-world UNIVAC, one of the earliest commercial computers. Asimov imagined its evolution from a room-sized machine to an entity that exists beyond space and time -- a vision that anticipates modern discussions about artificial superintelligence.
